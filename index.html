Before you go

Filipino passport holders need a visa for Japan — there's no visa-free entry, regardless of trip length. Start this early; it's the one item on this list with a real deadline.

Apply for a Japan tourist visa

Through JVAC (VFS Global — Manila, Cebu, or Davao). Visa itself is free for Filipinos; only the ~₱520 JVAC service fee applies. Apply 2–3 months before travel — a single-entry visa is valid 90 days from issue, so don't apply too early either.

Prepare visa documents

Passport (6+ months validity), a day-by-day itinerary with named hotels, proof of accommodation, bank certificate + 3–6 months of bank statements, and ITR or certificate of employment.

Book flights early

Spring is cherry blossom season — demand and prices climb. Budget carriers (Cebu Pacific, Jetstar Japan) and PAL/JAL/ANA all fly MNL–KIX direct; round trip runs roughly ₱12,000–19,000 if booked a few months out.

Book your hostel

Namba, Shinsaibashi, or Namba/Nishinari-ku edge (Shin-Imamiya, Dobutsuen-mae) are the cheapest, most central bases. Confirm the exact address before your visa application — it needs to match.

Get travel insurance

Not always required for the visa, but worth it for a group trip — covers delays, lost luggage, and medical costs.

Sort connectivity

A pocket wifi or eSIM (Klook/Airalo) for the group — split the cost 3–4 ways to keep it cheap per person.

Notify your bank

Flag your Philippine cards for international use if you plan to withdraw yen at 7-Eleven or FamilyMart ATMs.

Check Umeda Sky Building status

It's closed for renovation from Oct 2025 until roughly late April 2026 — confirm it's reopened before building it into your Amazing Pass day.

Morning
Osaka Castle & park

Entry covered by the Amazing Pass. If you're there in late March–April, the park's cherry blossoms are the main event.

Midday

Graze for lunch — grilled scallops, wagyu skewers, fresh sashimi by the piece.

Afternoon
Tsutenkaku Tower & Shinsekai

Tower entry covered by the pass. The neighborhood around it is Osaka's retro, slightly gritty old town.

Evening
Kushikatsu dinner in Shinsekai

Deep-fried skewers, cheap and filling — the local rule is no double-dipping in the shared sauce.

Nara Park · Todaiji Temple
Morning

JR Yamatoji Line or Kintetsu line, roughly 35–45 minutes each way.

Midday
Nara Park

Free-roaming deer that bow for shika senbei crackers (~¥200 a pack). Bring small bills.

Afternoon
Todaiji Temple

Entrance ~¥600 — home to one of the largest bronze Buddha statues in the world.

Return train, then a low-key dinner near your hostel.

~¥1,200 transport + ¥600 entrance + ~¥2,000 food
Alternative: swap Nara for a Kyoto day trip — Fushimi Inari's torii gates are free to walk, and Kiyomizu-dera is ~¥500. Kyoto is a longer ride (~30–45 min by JR/Hankyu) but doable in a day.

Universal Studios Japan

1-day pass ¥8,900–11,900 depending on the date. Book online in advance — physical ticket booths no longer sell same-day tickets. Go on a weekday if your dates allow it; it's noticeably cheaper.

Japan's tallest building's observatory (~¥2,000), Shitennoji Temple, and Sumiyoshi Taisha shrine (free) — a full day for a fraction of the USJ cost.

Packing for Osaka in spring

March–May swings from chilly mornings to warm afternoons, with a real chance of rain — Japan's spring is not all cherry blossoms and clear skies.

  • Layers — light jacket or cardigan for 8–15°C mornings, breathable tee underneath
  • A compact umbrella — spring showers are common and locals don't do ponchos
  • Comfortable walking shoes — expect 15,000+ steps a day between train stations and markets
  • A universal or Type-A plug adapter (Japan runs on 100V)
  • A small pouch of coins and small bills — many stalls and older shops are cash-only
  • Pocket tissues — public restrooms often don't stock paper towels or tissue
  • A reusable tote — plastic bags at convenience stores may cost extra
  • Allergy meds if pollen bothers you — spring is hay fever season in Kansai
  • A portable battery pack for maps and translation apps on long sightseeing days
  • One collared or slightly dressier outfit if you're doing a nicer dinner
  • Printed or downloaded copies of your visa, hotel booking, and flight itinerary
  • A daypack for the Nara/Kyoto day trip, separate from your main luggage

Money, apps, and small local rules

Cash still matters

Convenience stores and trains take cards/IC, but small izakayas, market stalls, and some temples are cash-only. Withdraw yen from 7-Eleven or FamilyMart ATMs — they reliably accept foreign cards.

Get an ICOCA card

Tap-to-ride on trains and buses, and it doubles as payment at convenience stores and vending machines. A small deposit is refundable when you return the card.

The Amazing Pass only pays off if you're moving

It covers Osaka Metro, city buses, and 40+ attractions — but only wins on a genuinely packed sightseeing day. A slow day of just wandering Dotonbori is cheaper without it.

Useful apps

Google Maps for transit routing, Google Translate (camera mode reads menus and signs on the spot), and Klook for booking USJ or Amazing Pass tickets ahead of time.

Split shared costs as a group

Pocket wifi, taxis for late-night returns, and even hostel private rooms get noticeably cheaper per person when split 3–4 ways instead of going solo.

Quiet-hours etiquette

Trains go silent-phone mode as the norm — calls are considered rude on board. Eating while walking through Dotonbori's food stalls is common, but it's frowned on elsewhere.
